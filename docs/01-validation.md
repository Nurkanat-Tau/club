# Stage 1 — Validation

> Status of every claim here: **hypothesis** unless marked **fact**. Nothing is proven until people show up twice.

## 1. My understanding of the idea

Club helps people in one city find a real-world group, join it, and keep coming back. Organizers get a simple way to run that group. The long-term bet is **density**: once a city has enough active clubs, Club becomes the default place to look for "something to do with people." That default spot could be monetized through organizers, paid events and local sponsors.

The first test is deliberately tiny: **Shymkent, 5 founding clubs, 4 weeks.**

## 2. The strongest version of the hypothesis

> **Young adults (18–30) in Shymkent who want to meet people through an activity don't join groups today. They can't easily see which groups exist, and walking into a stranger's WhatsApp chat feels awkward. A single page per club makes the first visit feel safe: what happens, when, who else is coming, and a one-tap "I'll come" button. If that page exists, more of them will attend, and enough will return to form communities.**

It becomes testable when we measure the parts separately:

| # | Hypothesis | What would prove it | What would kill it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | People want to find and join local groups | Strangers (not friends) join clubs through the link | Nearly all members are the organizer's existing friends |
| H2 | People who sign up actually come | ≥50% of "I'll come" turn into attendance | <35% show-up rate after reminders |
| H3 | They come back | ≥30% of first-time attendees attend again within 3 weeks | <10% return |
| H4 | Organizers will use the tool | 4 of 5 organizers create events themselves by week 2 | You have to create every event for them |
| H5 | Density creates cross-traffic | Some members join a second club | Zero overlap between clubs |

H5 is the one that makes this a platform rather than five separate groups. Watch it closely.

## 3. The biggest risks (ranked)

1. **"Why not just Instagram + WhatsApp?"** (highest risk). Organizers already have tools that work and cost nothing. If Club is only another link, they drop it after week 1. *Mitigation:* Club must remove work for them: RSVP counts, a copy-ready reminder text, one-tap WhatsApp to each person, attendance tracking. Chat stays in WhatsApp; we don't compete with it.
2. **Organizer consistency.** A club that skips two weeks dies. Five volunteers means some will fade. *Mitigation:* pick people who already run something weekly; recruit 1–2 backups.
3. **Friends-only traffic.** Your brother's audience joins because they like him, not because of Club. That tests nothing about discovery. *Mitigation:* track the `source` of each join and ask every new attendee, "How did you hear about us?"
4. **Discovery might not be the real pain.** Maybe people know groups exist but lack courage or time. The "who's going" list and "newcomers welcome" copy test this.
5. **Monetization is far away.** Community products monetize late. That's fine for now, but see §11 on why a $10/month subscription for users is the wrong first bet.
6. **Your time.** This competes with your teaching income. Cap it at ~10 hours/week for 4 weeks, then decide using the thresholds below.
7. **Safety and trust** (hiking especially): transport, weather, injuries, and minors. Publish a simple rule set and an 18+ policy for the pilot.

## 4. Answers to the 11 questions

**1. What exactly is the problem?**
For attendees, it's not knowing what groups exist in Shymkent and feeling awkward about showing up alone. For organizers, it's the manual work: tracking who's coming in DMs, no-shows, reminding people one by one, and not knowing who came back.

**2. Who is the first user?**
An 18–27-year-old in Shymkent: a student or young professional who is new to the city or bored with their routine. They're active on Instagram and already follow at least one local account. They want to do something regularly *and* meet people.

**3. Who is the first organizer?**
Your brother (running and walking). He has an audience he can invite. The ideal organizer already gathers people at least twice a month.

**4. Why would someone use Club instead of Instagram, Telegram or WhatsApp?**
- One page shows *what, when, where, and who else is coming*.
- One tap commits you. You don't have to DM a stranger.
- They can see all the clubs in their city in one place, which Instagram doesn't offer.

Be honest with yourself: **for one club, Instagram is enough.** Club only wins once there are several clubs in one place. That's why five clubs, not one.

**5. Why would an organizer use Club instead of their existing tools?**
- They see exactly who is coming, with WhatsApp links to remind each person.
- They get a copy-ready invite and reminder text.
- They get attendance and "who came back" numbers they don't have today.
- They get new people from the other four clubs' audiences.
- The founding-club status and our promotion are part of the deal (§10).

**6. What is our strongest initial use case?**
**The weekly run.** It happens every week, it's free, the barrier is low, it's social, it's visible on Instagram, and your brother already has distribution. One recurring event with an RSVP link in his bio is the cleanest test of H2 and H3.

**7. What should the five founding clubs be?**
See §5.

**8. What criteria should we use to select founders?**
See §7.

**9. How do I approach them?**
See §8 and `docs/02-founders.md`.

**10. What should I offer them?**
- **Free forever** for founding clubs.
- **Promotion:** a joint Instagram launch and cross-posting across all five audiences.
- **Tools** that save time (RSVPs, reminders, attendance).
- **Monthly numbers** on their community.
- **Founding-organizer status**, plus a say in what we build next.
- **No lock-in:** they keep their chat and their followers.

Don't promise money you don't have.

## 5. The five-club strategy

Principles:

- **Different days of the week**, so one person can attend two clubs. This tests H5.
- **At least 3 weekly clubs.** Recurring beats one-off.
- **A mix of free and outdoor with indoor** (weather is coming in Oct–Nov).
- **At least one club you run yourself**, so you use the product as an organizer every week.

| Slot | Club | Rhythm | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sat morning (+ Wed evening) | **Running / Walking**, your brother | Weekly | Existing audience, free, social, photogenic |
| Thu evening | **English Speaking Club**, *you* | Weekly | You have real expertise (IELTS 7.5, teacher). You'll feel organizer pain first-hand. Useful for students and professionals. **Don't host it at, or recruit from, your employer's center** (check your contract). |
| Sun afternoon | **Chess** (café or anti-café) | Weekly | Shymkent has an active chess scene (it hosted an open cup with GMs in 2024). Cheap venue, calm, good for introverts. |
| Tue / Fri evening | **Table tennis** | Weekly | Local table-tennis clubs are active on Instagram. The venue already exists; the club fills tables at off-peak hours. |
| Every 2nd Sunday | **Hiking / outdoor** | Biweekly | High emotional value and great content. Mountains and canyons are nearby (Aksu canyon, Sairam area). Riskier on logistics and safety. |

**Backup options if one founder drops out:** board games (very low barrier, indoor), volleyball (big amateur scene), book club.

**My recommendation:** if you can't find a reliable hiking organizer by day 5, replace hiking with **board games**. A weekly indoor club gives more data points than a biweekly outdoor trip.

## 6. The exact 7-day validation plan

Goal for the week: **5 committed organizers, 5 first events scheduled, and the first real event held.** The app is already built, so it's a tool in this plan, not the goal.

| Day | Actions | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Sit down with your brother for 60 minutes. Ask how he organizes today: what's annoying, how many people come, how he reminds them. Set up Supabase and Vercel (see README). Add his club. | Brother's club live; his real pain written down |
| **Day 2** | Build a list of 20 candidate organizers (see `02-founders.md`): search Instagram, 2GIS, university clubs and Telegram groups. Score each one against §7. Send 10 DMs using the script in §8. | 20 candidates, 10 messages sent |
| **Day 3** | Hold 3–5 calls or meetings. Use problem questions, not a pitch: "How do you gather people now? Last event, how many came? How do you remind them?" Pitch only in the last 5 minutes. | 3–5 conversations logged |
| **Day 4** | Send 10 more DMs. Follow up with yesterday's calls. Get **verbal yes from 2 organizers** + schedule their first event. Run your own English club's first event date. | 3 clubs committed |
| **Day 5** | Close the remaining 2 organizers or the backups. Create organizer accounts. Sit with each organizer for 15 minutes while they create their first event themselves. That tests H4. | 5 clubs, 5 events in the app |
| **Day 6** | **The brother's run happens.** Before it, he posts the link. After it, he marks attendance in the app, and you ask 5 attendees three questions: how they found it, would they come again, and what almost stopped them. | First real attendance data |
| **Day 7** | Review with the `/admin` dashboard. Write down what surprised you. Decide the week-2 focus. Post "we're launching 5 clubs in Shymkent" on your Instagram. | Week-1 review in the Founder OS project |

**Kill signal already in week 1:** if fewer than 3 of 20 candidate organizers are interested, H4 is weak. Rethink the organizer offer before building anything else.

## 7. Criteria for choosing the four additional founding organizers

Score each candidate from 0 to 3 on every criterion. Only take people with a **total ≥ 14 and no zero on the first three.**

| Criterion | 0 | 3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Already gathers people regularly** | Never has | Runs something at least every 2 weeks |
| **Reliability** | Flaky replies, cancels | Answers fast, keeps promises |
| **Real local community** | Followers are mostly not from Shymkent | Comments and faces from Shymkent |
| Engagement (not follower count) | Likes only | Real comments, DMs, people tagging friends |
| Can bring the first 10 people alone | No | Yes, easily |
| Venue or logistics solved | No place to meet | Has a place or a route |
| Trust and credibility | Unknown | Coach, champion, teacher, known face |
| Wants to grow the community | Doesn't care | Actively wants new people |

**Red flags:** only wants money up front; wants the club to be an ad for their paid course; audience is mostly bots or out-of-city; not able to commit to 4 weeks.

A 2,000-follower local coach with 40 people at a weekly event beats a 50,000-follower lifestyle blogger.

## 8. How to approach them (script)

**First DM (Russian; switch to Kazakh if they write in Kazakh):**

> Сәлем, [Имя]! Меня зовут Нурканат, я из Шымкента. Вижу, что вы [проводите игры / тренировки / походы] — классно, что у вас живое сообщество.
> Мы запускаем в Шымкенте проект Club: 5 клубов-основателей, у каждого своя страница, запись на встречи в один клик, напоминания и статистика — бесплатно. Плюс совместное продвижение между клубами.
> Можно 15 минут созвониться или встретиться на этой неделе? Хочу сначала понять, как вы сейчас собираете людей.

**On the call (roughly 15 minutes of listening, 5 of pitching):**

1. "How do you gather people now? Walk me through the last event."
2. "How many signed up, and how many actually came?"
3. "How do you remind people? How long does it take?"
4. "What's the most annoying part of running this?"
5. "Where do new members come from?"
6. *Then:* show them the app on your phone (the organizer screen) for 2 minutes.
7. **Ask for a concrete commitment:** "Can we put your next event in Club and post the link on [date]?"

## 9. Metrics and decision thresholds (4-week pilot)

Definitions (calculated on `/admin`):

- **Show rate** = attended ÷ "going" RSVPs on past events where attendance was marked
- **Return rate** = members who attended ≥ 2 events ÷ members who attended ≥ 1
- **Activation** = members who attended ≥ 1 ÷ all members
- **Active club** = held an event in the last 14 days or has an upcoming one
- **Organizer retention** = organizers still creating events in week 4 ÷ 5

| Metric | Week-4 target (continue) | Warning | Stop signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active clubs | 5/5 (≥ 4 ok) | 3 | ≤ 2 |
| Events held | ≥ 3 per club (≥ 15 total) | 8–14 | < 8 |
| Members | ≥ 150 total | 60–150 | < 60 |
| Show rate | ≥ 50% | 35–50% | < 35% after reminders |
| Return rate | ≥ 30% | 15–30% | < 10% |
| Members from outside the organizers' own circle | ≥ 40% | 20–40% | < 20% |
| Members in 2+ clubs | ≥ 10% | 3–10% | 0 |
| Organizers creating events themselves | ≥ 4 | 3 | ≤ 2 |
| Average event rating | ≥ 4.2 | 3.5–4.2 | < 3.5 |

**Decision rules:**

- **A) Continue:** at least 4 clubs with 3+ events, ≥150 members, show rate ≥50% and return rate ≥30%. *Next step:* add 5 more clubs in Shymkent and test the first money (§11).
- **B) Change the concept:** people attend and come back, but they don't use the app (they RSVP in DMs) and organizers ignore it. *Next step:* Club becomes an organizer tool or a media or curation brand ("what to do in Shymkent this week"), not a platform.
- **C) Change the target audience:** members come mostly from one segment (for example university students, or expats), or one club type dominates. *Next step:* narrow to that segment, such as a "student clubs" platform.
- **D) Change the business model:** strong usage, but organizers refuse any paid feature later. *Next step:* test sponsors or paid events instead of subscriptions.
- **E) Stop:** at most 2 active clubs by week 3, return rate below 10%, or you can't find 3 willing organizers. Write down what you learned and move to the next problem on the Problem Map.

## 11. Business model: is $10/month right?

**Not for users, and not now.**

- $10/month is roughly ₸5,000, which is a meaningful share of a student's budget in Shymkent (my estimate; check current exchange rates). Most groups are free, and people won't pay to *find* a free run.
- Subscriptions for discovery only work after you have lots of clubs and exclusive value. Club has neither yet.
- More promising order to test, **only after the pilot works**:
  1. **Transaction fee on paid events** (hikes, tournaments, workshops). Money is already changing hands. Take 5–10% via Kaspi links.
  2. **Organizer Pro** (≈ ₸3–5k/month) for clubs that charge members: payments, member subscriptions, waitlists, analytics. Test this by asking, not building.
  3. **Local sponsors** (coffee shops, sports stores, gyms) pay for exposure to an active, local, young audience. Five active clubs can already be a sellable package.
  4. **A user subscription** only much later, for example a "Club Pass" with discounts at partner venues.

**Cheapest test of willingness to pay (week 4):** ask the hiking and table-tennis organizers, "If Club collected payments and handled no-shows, would you pay ₸3,000 a month or give 7% of paid events?" Count real yeses. Then ask for a pre-payment.
